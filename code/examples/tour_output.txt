Structure-provenance tour: rule-based agents with known sources of differentiation, 5 runs per condition.
Each value is repeatability R (the share of between-member differences that persists across windows),
read against the 95th percentile of its own label-permutation null. Simulating........ done in 97 s.

1. IS THERE STRUCTURE?  Does task specialization persist beyond chance?
  No source (identical agents)                 task-type R 0.09 vs baseline 0.19   above in 0 of 5 runs
  Specialties prescribed in the agents' rules  task-type R 0.57 vs baseline 0.20   above in 5 of 5 runs
  Specialties learned from own history         task-type R 0.38 vs baseline 0.19   above in 5 of 5 runs
  -> The measure stays at chance when there is nothing to find and detects both real sources.
     But both sources look alike here: persistence alone cannot say where structure came from.

2. IS IT ROBUST?  Agents prefer whoever carries the label 'A1'. Does the hub survive relabeling?
  Fixed names                                  hub R       0.97 vs baseline 0.19   above in 5 of 5 runs
  Balanced label shuffle                       hub R       0.10 vs baseline 0.19   above in 0 of 5 runs
  -> A persistent communication hub that disappears when labels are balanced was produced by the
     design (a label cue), not by an organizing process among the agents.

3. WHERE DID IT COME FROM?  Wipe every agent's history at round 50, then ask whether agents keep their
   favorite task type across round 50 (all agents of all runs pooled; chance = favorites permuted).
  Learned, no wipe                             0.49 of agents keep their favorite (chance 0.27; p = 0.001)
  Learned, history wiped                       0.31 of agents keep their favorite (chance 0.25; p = 0.189)
  Prescribed (reassigned each run), no wipe    0.96 of agents keep their favorite (chance 0.26; p < 0.001)
  Prescribed (reassigned each run), wiped      0.96 of agents keep their favorite (chance 0.26; p < 0.001)
  Drop in continuity caused by the wipe, learned    (paired by seed): 0.18 [95% CI 0.09, 0.27]
  Drop in continuity caused by the wipe, prescribed (paired by seed): 0.00 [95% CI 0.00, 0.00]
  -> The wipe returns history-dependent continuity toward chance but leaves prescribed roles intact:
     an intervention separates two sources that persistence alone could not.

The 'structural' (hub) column is above baseline in every fixed-name run, with the same member on top,
and at chance under balanced labels. The 'relational' column can stay above baseline in both: agents
still pair by label position, which is why the analysis package adds a label-aware partner baseline.
To analyze your own simulation or group, write a CSV with the same columns (run, time, member, action,
partner, task_type, optional opportunity) and pass it with --csv; `provenance_report.py --help` documents it.
